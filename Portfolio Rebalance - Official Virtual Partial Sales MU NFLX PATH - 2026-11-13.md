## Why I am making these sales

### Micron: keep the memory thesis, reduce the cycle risk

I still believe AI demand can make memory, especially HBM, more strategically important. That is why I am keeping half the position.

My concern is the price I am paying for the next several years of earnings. Micron has been benefiting from exceptional memory economics. Those earnings can be real and still be near a cyclical high. As the industry adds capacity, stronger supply could bring pricing and margins back down, even if long-term AI memory demand keeps growing.

I do not have enough confidence that the current level of profitability will hold through the next supply response. The potential alpha does not justify a full $4,000 position for me at the moment. I am reducing it to $2,000 and keeping exposure in case the structural HBM case proves stronger than the historical cycle.

### Netflix: smaller position until I can value the franchise upside with more confidence

My original investment was not just a streaming bet. It was a bet that Netflix could use its existing global audience and distribution to build more valuable franchises, ads and other entertainment businesses over time. I still think that can happen.

What has changed is how confidently I can price that upside. A show can take years to produce and be consumed in a weekend. Publishing more content overall does not mean each subscriber gets more of the content they personally want. Major hits and durable franchises are also harder to forecast than demand for enterprise software or semiconductor capacity.

The stock's valuation is not unreasonable, which is why I am not exiting. But I would rather hold $5,000 than $7,500 until I have a better model for repeat viewing, retention, advertising economics and long-lived IP value.

### UiPath: the agentic automation opportunity is real; the proof of acceleration is not strong enough yet

I still see a place for UiPath in coordinating AI agents and the business processes around them. Existing enterprise automation relationships are worth something.

The issue is whether UiPath captures enough of that opportunity to beat the market by a meaningful margin. ARR growth and retention have not yet shown the step-change I would want, while Microsoft, Salesforce and other platforms are building overlapping functionality. A good market does not automatically mean an exceptional equity return for every participant.

I am cutting the position in half rather than selling out. I want to see sustained acceleration in recurring revenue, better net retention and stronger cash generation before committing more capital again.

## What stays unchanged

I am **not selling Synopsys**. EDA remains a hard-to-replace part of semiconductor design. There are legitimate questions around the Ansys integration, capital efficiency and valuation, but I am keeping the $5,000 position while those play out.

I am **not selling Mastercard**. Its network economics, existing cash generation and relative independence from the AI infrastructure spending cycle make it useful in this portfolio. The $7,877 nominal USD position stays intact. The historical original allocation was €7,000.

Nothing else is sold or bought in this decision.
