## Why I am buying again

My October 29 sale was a tactical response to the market's initial earnings reaction. It was **not** a conclusion that Amazon's long-term business or AI thesis had become worthless.

The lower share price improves the prospective entry valuation relative to the price environment at my sale, assuming the company's future cash-generating capacity has not deteriorated by an equivalent amount. That is the reason for the re-entry. I am not claiming to have identified the bottom or that a decline alone guarantees positive alpha.

The Q3 release illustrates both sides of the investment:

- **Operating strength:** Quarterly revenue reached **$201.9 billion**, operating income **$28.1 billion**, and AWS net sales grew **39.3% year over year**. The operating margin reached **13.9%**.
- **Capital-conversion risk:** Amazon reaffirmed expected **2026 cash capital expenditure of approximately $220 billion**. Its reported trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was **negative $42.4 billion**. Infrastructure spending, depreciation, utilization and future returns on invested capital remain the critical variables.
- **Market expectations:** The post-report selloff reflects concerns about near-term earnings, guidance and the scale of the AI investment cycle. Strong AWS growth does not automatically mean that the market's previous earnings and cash-flow expectations were justified.

My broader view remains that Amazon has an unusually large base of productive assets and expansion opportunities across AWS, custom silicon, advertising, commerce, logistics and robotics. The potential comes from turning those investments into sustainable economic returns, not merely from spending more on AI. The risks I identified before the sale are still present; the new decision is based on a **changed price**, not on pretending that those risks disappeared.
