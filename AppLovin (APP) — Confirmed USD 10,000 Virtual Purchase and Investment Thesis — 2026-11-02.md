## The decision

I have bought AppLovin in my virtual portfolio with a **$10,000 allocation**.

I am not buying it because its share price fell, because advertising uses AI, or because its reported margins look spectacular in isolation. I am buying because I think the share price may be discounting a more severe decline in the economics of AppLovin's advertising platform than is necessary for the investment to outperform the S&P 500 over several years.

That judgment can be wrong. The company has a concentrated business model, depends on platforms it does not control and faces specific legal and regulatory uncertainty. Nevertheless, the combination of current cash-generating capacity, valuation and plausible persistence of its operating advantage is strong enough for me to allocate meaningful capital rather than open a token position.

The $10,000 amount also matters. My research portfolio has historically included larger allocations to companies such as SAP, Salesforce, Synopsys and Meta and smaller allocations to more uncertain names. AppLovin does not deserve the same *risk classification* as a diversified software incumbent merely because it is currently more profitable. But the expected return is sufficiently attractive that I am allocating more than the $7,000 minimum I considered. I chose $10,000 rather than automatically matching the portfolio's largest historical $12,500 positions.

## What the business does, and where the economics come from

AppLovin operates an AI-driven advertising marketplace and optimization platform. Its AXON technology attempts to predict which advertising impression is likely to produce the highest economic outcome for an advertiser, subject to campaign goals and the price of that impression.

The mechanism is straightforward in principle:

1. Better prediction improves the conversion value an advertiser receives for each dollar spent.
2. Higher measured return on advertising spend makes the platform more attractive to advertisers and can support additional budgets.
3. More useful activity can improve the platform's ability to optimize future matching, provided the underlying data remain accessible and the feedback is informative.
4. If the optimization improvement is defensible, AppLovin may retain some of the resulting value through revenue growth and high incremental margins.

Each link is conditional. Model improvement has no durable value to shareholders if it does not improve customer economics, if competitors reproduce it cheaply, if the platform loses the data or distribution required to operate, or if most of the surplus is competed away.

What interests me is not the phrase 'AI advertising'. It is the possibility that a comparatively small improvement in allocating a very large pool of advertising spending can create material economic value without a corresponding increase in operating expense.

The most important evidence will be sustained spending and retention among advertisers, actual revenue growth, margins and free cash flow. A claimed uplift in model quality is not a substitute for those results.

## Why I think the valuation may be too pessimistic

The last independently verified regular-session close preceding this decision was **$228.50 on October 30, 2026**, down 10.36% that day from $254.91 on October 29. The price decline changes the amount I pay for the business; it does not by itself prove that the business is undervalued.

For a transparent reverse valuation I use an **illustrative 2026 diluted EPS starting assumption of $15.71**. This is a *model assumption*, not a management EPS forecast, a verified full-year result or a guaranteed analyst consensus. It implies roughly **14.5 times that assumed 2026 EPS** at the $228.50 reference price.

The only question the model answers is: if 2026 EPS were $15.71 and earnings grew at different rates for four years, what terminal multiple would be sufficient to beat a nominal 9% annual S&P 500 total-return assumption?

I use:

```text
2030 estimated share price = 2026 assumed EPS × (1 + annual EPS growth)^4 × 2030 P/E
APP annualized price return = (2030 estimated share price / 228.50)^(1/4) − 1
Annualized alpha spread = APP annualized return − 9% benchmark assumption
```

| Case | Assumed annual EPS growth, 2026–2030 | 2030 P/E | Calculated 2030 price | Annualized APP return | Spread vs. 9% benchmark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Severe disappointment | 4% | 11 | $202.16 | −3.02% | −12.02 pp |
| Weak | 8% | 12 | $256.48 | +2.93% | −6.07 pp |
| Central | 14% | 14 | $371.47 | +12.92% | **+3.92 pp** |
| Strong | 20% | 17 | $553.80 | +24.77% | +15.77 pp |
| Very strong | 25% | 20 | $767.09 | +35.36% | +26.36 pp |

The central result is often summarized as **approximately +4 percentage points of annual alpha**. That is a *scenario-based working estimate*, not demonstrated predictive skill or an econometrically established expected return. The five scenarios have not been assigned defensible empirical probabilities; they must not be averaged as though they formed a calibrated distribution. The 9% S&P 500 assumption is a hurdle, not a claim about the market's actual return over the next four years.

The central case requires 14% annual EPS growth and a terminal P/E of 14. Both are far below the latest reported year-on-year growth rates, which is precisely what makes the setup interesting. However, **growth deceleration is not the same as durable growth**. The difference between 14% and 8% EPS growth, combined with a 14 versus 12 terminal multiple, is enough to move the thesis from meaningful modeled alpha to underperformance. The apparent valuation cushion must therefore be tested against real business deterioration.

I do not treat $371.47 as a precise price forecast. The formula excludes dividends, tax, transaction costs, currency effects and the timing of future capital distributions. It also assumes a stable connection between reported EPS and economic earnings. The next quarterly report can change the starting EPS estimate materially.

## What would make AXON a durable advantage?

I see three possible sources of persistence:

**Prediction quality.** Better estimates of advertiser value per impression could improve campaign performance, producing an advantage that is economically measurable rather than merely technical.

**Marketplace depth.** A broader and more useful set of advertisers, publishers and campaign observations could improve allocation efficiency. This feedback loop is valuable only if customers and partners remain willing to participate and the platform can lawfully use the required information.

**High incremental profitability.** Once the core infrastructure and models exist, additional advertising activity may cost much less to serve than the revenue it generates. The existing 84% adjusted EBITDA margin is evidence of an unusually productive operating model, but not proof that margins will remain there indefinitely.

I would reject the strong version of the moat thesis if model improvements stop translating into advertiser returns, retention or revenue; if competitors close the performance gap; if access to critical data changes; or if customer-acquisition costs rise enough to absorb the incremental economics.

Expansion beyond the platform's established mobile-app advertising base is upside potential, not revenue already earned. The investment does not require me to assume that every new customer category will succeed.

## The risks I am actually accepting

**Concentrated economics.** AppLovin relies heavily on the performance of its advertising technology. A deterioration in AXON's relative effectiveness could affect revenue, margins and valuation simultaneously. It has less diversification than Microsoft, Alphabet or Amazon.

**Dependence on outside platforms.** Changes in privacy rules, attribution, data availability, app-store policies or platform-partner agreements can affect the ability to target, measure and monetize advertising. Apple, Google and Meta can alter the operating environment without needing AppLovin's consent.

**Legal and regulatory exposure.** AppLovin's June 2026 Form 10-Q discusses securities litigation and possible regulatory proceedings. Reuters reported in February 2026 that an SEC investigation involving AppLovin remained active, citing Bloomberg. The SEC had not publicly accused AppLovin or its executives of wrongdoing, and Reuters could not independently verify the report. A lawsuit and an investigation are risk factors, not findings of guilt. I do not assign them a made-up probability or assume the exposure has disappeared.

**Earnings quality and shareholder economics.** Strong adjusted EBITDA does not settle how much cash is available to shareholders. I will track GAAP income, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, tax payments, stock compensation, debt, net diluted shares and actual capital returns. Repurchases can be valuable, but they can also destroy value if executed at excessive prices.

**Expectation and multiple compression.** A high-margin company can lose a large share of its market value without its revenue turning negative. Investors may decide that the profit level is unsustainable, forecast materially weaker earnings and apply a lower multiple at the same time. My severe case is not a maximum-loss bound. The stock can fall further.

**Correlated portfolio exposure.** Although AppLovin has different customers and economics, owning it alongside Meta and Alphabet increases exposure to the economics and regulation of digital advertising. A broad repricing of AI-related earnings expectations could also affect several of my holdings together.

## Why the allocation is $10,000

My allocation decision is relative to the rest of the **virtual** portfolio, not an abstract rule that every company receiving a 'buy' gets the same amount.

Historical allocations used in our comparison included approximately $12,500 each in SAP, Salesforce and UiPath, $11,000 in Synopsys, $10,000 in Meta and Wolters Kluwer, $9,000 in Cadence, and smaller initial allocations in Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft. Some of these positions have subsequently been changed or exited; the historical figures are **not** a verified current holdings statement. In particular, Cadence was later exited and Synopsys trimmed. My risk comparison does not silently treat those earlier allocations as live positions.

AppLovin's business is presently generating outstanding profits. That supports a substantial position. But the durability of its profit source is less certain than for a diversified cash-generating franchise. I therefore do not size it larger than the biggest historical positions merely because the modeled alpha is positive.

I considered $7,000 a minimum and chose exactly $10,000. At that capital amount:

- A 30% decline would reduce the position by **$3,000**.
- A 50% decline would reduce it by **$5,000**.
- A 70% decline would reduce it by **$7,000**.

These are arithmetic stress tests, not probabilities. The potential loss from an unleveraged equity position can approach the full $10,000. The $10,000 allocation is a meaningful expression of conviction, constrained by the possibility that the underlying advantage or earnings base is less durable than it appears.

Against **Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Synopsys and Wolters Kluwer**, I judge AppLovin's idiosyncratic risk to be higher. Against **Micron and SK Hynix**, risk is high but of a different kind: memory producers have more pronounced commodity-supply and pricing cycles; AppLovin has greater model-, partner- and regulatory-dependence. Against **UiPath and Pegasystems**, a simple ranking is less robust because the businesses face different commercialization, competition and valuation risks.

This is why I can simultaneously believe AppLovin offers better modeled alpha than several current holdings and refuse to classify it as a lower-risk investment. **Expected return and confidence in that expectation are different variables.**

I am not claiming an exact portfolio percentage. That would require a reconciled current total including recent additions, sales, cash and foreign-exchange conversions.

## How I will test whether the investment was justified

The investment is falsifiable. I will preserve the November 2 assumptions and test them against later outcomes, rather than rewriting the thesis after the fact.

I will monitor:

1. **Growth:** quarterly advertising revenue, year-on-year trends and the difference between management guidance and realized outcomes. The central valuation case needs a credible route to approximately 14% compounded EPS growth after 2026, not just a strong current quarter.
2. **Model productivity:** evidence that AXON improvements create lasting advertiser return on spend and translate into spending, rather than temporary uplift or changes in measurement.
3. **Margins and cash conversion:** adjusted EBITDA versus GAAP results, operating cash flow, free cash flow, taxes, stock compensation and the diluted share count. These are complementary checks, not substitutes.
4. **Platform and legal developments:** changes to partner policies and attribution, and verified developments in disclosed investigations and litigation.
5. **Institutional distribution:** Q3 and subsequent 13F share-count changes, especially coordinated reductions by major holders. Reporting delays and changes in beneficial-owner aggregation must be recognized.
6. **Valuation:** an updated scenario grid using the share price and information available on each review date. A falling stock price alone is neither confirmation nor refutation of the thesis.

For performance evaluation I will compare a fully specified portfolio entry and later market value with the **S&P 500 total return** in the same currency and for the same dates. The price reference of $228.50 must not be silently promoted into a verified November 2 fill. Until an exact simulated fill convention or execution quote is recorded, the $10,000 allocation is confirmed but share-level performance attribution remains provisional.

## What would change my mind?

A weaker quarter does not automatically destroy a multi-year thesis. Repeatedly disappointing growth, a declining ability to turn incremental ad spending into cash, a persistent loss of AXON's measured advantage, material new restrictions on platform access or adverse evidence about reported economics would.

I would also reconsider the allocation if the price rises so far that reasonable growth no longer produces a meaningful spread over the benchmark. A great business can become a poor investment at the wrong price. Conversely, a lower price does not justify adding if the cash-flow outlook has deteriorated faster.

The first near-term checkpoint is **November 4, 2026**, when AppLovin is scheduled to publish Q3 results. I will assess it against this dated decision rather than treat an earnings surprise as something I already knew.
