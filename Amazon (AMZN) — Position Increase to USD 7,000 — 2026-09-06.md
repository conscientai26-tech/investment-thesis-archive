## Why I am increasing the position

I am increasing Amazon from USD 3,000 to USD 7,000.

The first USD 3,000 position was intentionally small. At the time I had moved from no position to a starter position because I had become more positive on the thesis, but I still treated a large part of the upside as dependent on a fairly speculative idea: Amazon eventually becoming something close to a generalized deployment machine.

That framing has changed.

I no longer think Amazon needs one huge future transformation for the investment to work. The more useful way to think about the company is as a resource-heavy expansion machine that already owns several large economic systems and can reuse them as AI changes what is scarce.

AWS, logistics, advertising, marketplace infrastructure, custom silicon, robotics and internal operating data are not independent bets. They give Amazon several places where AI can improve productivity, lower coordination costs, create new products or help the company respond when a new bottleneck appears.

The important change in my view is therefore not simply that I am more bullish on AI. It is that I now see a less binary path from today's Amazon to a more valuable Amazon.

The company does not need to become the dominant model provider. It does not need to win every infrastructure layer. It only needs to keep converting its existing assets, capital and operating feedback into new productive capacity at attractive returns.

That fits much better with the framework I am now using across the portfolio:

**scarce core / existing economic base → expansion surface → bottleneck response → capital conversion → shareholder cash flow.**

Amazon has a very large existing economic base and unusually broad expansion surface. The unresolved question is still capital conversion, not whether the company has enough opportunities to invest into.

## The second reason: the old USD 3,000 size no longer makes sense relative to the rest of the portfolio

Position size should reflect relative conviction, not the order in which ideas happened to enter the portfolio.

After comparing Amazon with the other positions, USD 3,000 looks too small.

I currently give USD 5,000 positions to much less proven businesses or theses such as PEGA and Ambiq. Those positions are intentionally small because their upside is interesting but their long-term economics are much less certain.

Amazon is not lower-conviction than those companies.

It has a much larger realized economic base, much stronger financing capacity, multiple already proven businesses and a much wider set of ways in which AI can create value. Keeping Amazon below those positions would therefore make the portfolio sizing internally inconsistent.

At the same time, I do not think Amazon deserves the same size as the highest-conviction positions yet. WKL at USD 10,000, for example, currently has a much larger valuation cushion. Amazon still requires enormous capital investment and the central question remains whether the current AI and infrastructure buildout eventually produces sufficiently high incremental returns.

That makes USD 7,000 a better middle position.

It says:

- Amazon is no longer just an exploratory idea.
- The thesis is stronger and less binary than when I opened the USD 3,000 starter.
- The position should be larger than the small option-like positions.
- But the capital-conversion question is not resolved enough to justify a full high-conviction allocation.

## What changed in the AI view

The biggest conceptual change is that I now think the AI transition should be analyzed as a sequence of moving bottlenecks rather than as one static technology bet.

More compute can create scarcity in memory, networking, power, cooling, construction or grid access. Solving one of those constraints allows the system to expand until another constraint becomes binding.

Amazon is interesting in this framework because it does not only sit on one side of that chain.

It owns enough infrastructure, capital, software, distribution and real-world operations to react to several different bottlenecks.

Trainium can reduce dependence on external accelerators for some workloads. AWS can absorb new compute demand. Logistics and robotics give Amazon a physical deployment surface. Marketplace and advertising give it large demand and monetization systems. Internal operations generate real-world friction data that can improve software, forecasting and automation.

The stronger version of the thesis is not "Amazon owns every bottleneck."

It is:

**Amazon may be unusually good at identifying the next bottleneck, allocating capital toward it, learning from deployment and then reusing the resulting capability across several businesses.**

If that is true, the company has a compounding advantage that is broader than AWS growth alone.

This is still not something I treat as fully proven. But it is now strong enough that a USD 3,000 allocation understates the thesis.

## Why I am not going straight to USD 10,000 or more

The major risk has not disappeared.

Amazon is entering one of the most capital-intensive phases in its history. A company can be strategically correct and still create mediocre equity returns if each new dollar of growth requires too much reinvestment.

The key chain remains:

**capital → infrastructure → utilization → revenue → operating profit → free cash flow → attractive incremental return on capital.**

If Amazon keeps building faster than the resulting cash flows justify, the larger strategic thesis does not rescue the investment.

There are also other risks:

- compute economics can commoditize;
- depreciation and replacement requirements can remain structurally high;
- power, memory, packaging, construction or grid constraints can capture part of the economics;
- internal complexity can offset some of the productivity gains from AI;
- competitors can benefit from the same decline in coordination and software costs;
- and the starting market value is already enormous.

So the increase to USD 7,000 is not a statement that the thesis is solved.

It is a statement that the previous position had become too small relative to both the quality of the business and my updated understanding of the AI transition.

---

## What would justify another increase

I would consider moving Amazon above USD 7,000 if several things become clearer at the same time:

- AWS and AI infrastructure growth translate into operating cash flow fast enough to offset the investment wave;
- free cash flow recovers while Amazon continues expanding capacity;
- Trainium shows real economic advantage rather than merely strategic independence;
- AI materially improves internal productivity or deployment speed;
- robotics learning becomes reusable across more tasks and locations;
- Amazon converts internal capabilities into external products or services with attractive margins;
- and the stock price does not already capitalize most of that improvement.

I would also increase if the share price fell materially while these underlying conditions remained intact, because the expected return on new capital would improve.

USD 7,000 better reflects that conviction without pretending that the central capital-conversion risk has been resolved.
