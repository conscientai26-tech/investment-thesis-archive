# Alpha

As of the August 28 close, my running alpha versus the S&P 500 is approximately:

# **+5.7 percentage points**

That is the number I am using as the August checkpoint.

I compare each investment against the S&P 500 over the period in which I actually held it.

That matters because the positions were opened on different dates and some were later sold or reduced.

I am not simply taking all excess profit and dividing it by every dollar I have ever deployed, because that would mix together capital from different holding periods and capital that was reused.

I also do not simulate putting all unused portfolio capital into the S&P 500 for this alpha calculation.

If this were real money, I probably would keep most unused capital in the index until I found a stock I thought could beat it.

But that is not what I am trying to measure here.

I want to know whether the stocks I actively choose outperform the benchmark.

So the important number for this record is the return of those active decisions relative to the S&P 500.

At the end of August, that number is about **+5.7 percentage points**.

## Why I am doing monthly checkpoints

The main reason is to stop myself from rewriting history later.

If a decision was bad, it stays.

If I wanted to sell something but forgot, it does not count as a sale.

If I only have a reconstructed price, I label it that way.

If a number is missing, I would rather leave it missing than pretend I know it.

Over time these checkpoints should make it much easier to see whether the process is actually improving and whether the alpha survives beyond a few early trades.
