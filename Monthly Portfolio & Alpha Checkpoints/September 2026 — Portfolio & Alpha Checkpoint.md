## Xiaomi

I sold Xiaomi completely on September 3 at HKD 27.44.

The original reference price was HKD 27.76.

That gives an approximate loss of **1.15% before fees and FX**, or about **-$173** on the $15,000 virtual position.

The more important part of that trade was what changed in my thinking.

My original Xiaomi thesis relied heavily on its strong price-performance position bringing users into the ecosystem.

After looking more closely at the business, I became less comfortable with how dependent that advantage was on component costs and on Xiaomi successfully moving from a low-price hardware advantage toward a deeper ecosystem advantage.

The ecosystem thesis may still work. I just no longer thought the path was proven enough to keep the position.

One rule I took from this is that I should not treat a moat as a fixed label.

I need to ask what actually creates the advantage, what it depends on, and whether the company can move into a new advantage if the old one weakens.

## What changed in the process

The biggest change since August is that I am more careful about whether different stocks are actually different bets.

Several companies can look diversified on the ticker level while depending on the same underlying idea.

SAP and Wolters Kluwer, for example, both depend partly on the idea that AI may weaken some outer software or information layers without destroying the value of trusted data, workflow, state and execution underneath them.

If that idea is wrong, both positions can be hit at the same time.

So I now care more about thesis correlation, not just the number of companies I own.

I also became more comfortable using small positions when I like a thesis but do not trust my own model enough for a normal-sized position.

Pegasystems is a good example. I like the structural idea, but the financial proof is still incomplete.

Netflix is another example. I like the company and the thesis, but I know that I understand enterprise software, AI infrastructure and bottlenecks much better than I understand entertainment economics. The $6,000 size reflects that.

I also formalized the five investment theses I have been using:

- Scarce-Core Expansion
- Catch-up Giants
- Bottleneck Asymmetry
- Deep / Railway Bottlenecks
- Bottleneck Migration

I do not treat those as formulas that automatically produce a buy decision. They are mainly a way to force myself to ask where scarcity moves, who captures the value, and whether the market already expects it.

of running alpha versus the S&P 500.

Using the same continuity method, the current estimate through the September 21 close is approximately:

So the portfolio gave back a meaningful part of the early lead.

That is worth recording.

The point of these checkpoints is not to preserve the highest number I ever reached. It is to make it harder to rewrite the track record later.

The alpha calculation still follows the same rule as before.

I compare each investment with the S&P 500 over the period in which I actually held it.

Sold or trimmed capital stops at the recorded exit or trim.

If capital is later reused, I do not count it twice.

I also do not include the unused part of the $1 million reference portfolio in the alpha calculation.

The number is meant to answer one question:

**Did the stocks I actively chose outperform the S&P 500 while I held them?**

It is not meant to simulate the return of a fully invested real-money $1 million portfolio.
