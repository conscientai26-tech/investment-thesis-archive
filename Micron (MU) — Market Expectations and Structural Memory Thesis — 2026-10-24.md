## The key question

After a reported gain of more than 300% in the prior year, Micron could still appear inexpensive relative to projected earnings. The relevant question is not simply whether AI memory demand will grow, but whether **sustainable cash generation after the current pricing boom** exceeds what investors currently discount.

## Why an apparently low forward P/E is ambiguous

The earlier analysis cited FY2026 DRAM selling-price growth of approximately 180% and NAND price growth of approximately 200%, versus memory bit shipments rising approximately 25% in each category. If confirmed, this suggests the profit surge was disproportionately price-driven rather than purely volume-driven.

Two interpretations remain possible:

1. **Structural repricing:** AI-related demand, constrained supply, and longer-term contracts sustain higher memory margins than historical cycles.
2. **Peak-earnings trap:** unusually high selling prices normalize as supply catches up, causing earnings and the multiple's apparent cheapness to disappear.

The thesis is not that a memory downcycle is impossible. It is that the depth, timing, and persistence of the next downcycle may differ materially from historical patterns.

## Potential sources of differentiated expectations

**Personal AI:** Persistent, local and cloud-coordinated agents could create more continuously active memory demand than conventional one-off smartphone upgrades. Model incremental RAM per device, adoption rates, concurrent agents and refresh cycles rather than assuming every personal-AI deployment generates the same memory footprint.

**Inference across memory tiers:** AI deployment could raise demand for HBM, server DRAM, LPDDR and SSD capacity. Efficiency gains can offset per-task memory needs, so distinguish growth in total workloads from memory used per workload.

**Contract structure and margins:** The strongest possible valuation impact may come from how much revenue is protected by long-term price arrangements and whether that protection translates into sustained gross margins and FCF. Public announcements of agreements do not by themselves prove company-wide price floors.

**Expectations gap:** Public discussion already includes personal AI, AI workstations and inference. An edge would have to concern their *magnitude, timing, or persistence*, rather than simply recognizing those trends.

## Risk and portfolio fit

A stock can be attractive fundamentally but still be an unsuitable addition to a portfolio concentrated in AI and technology. The existing strategy had generated approximately **10 percentage points of alpha versus the S&P 500** according to the investor's running calculation. As a simple sensitivity example, a 10% portfolio weight in Micron followed by a 50% Micron drawdown, with the benchmark and all other holdings flat, would subtract approximately **5 percentage points** of relative return. Correlated drawdowns in other AI holdings could amplify losses.

A decision should therefore be based on expected *portfolio-level, risk-adjusted* alpha, not only on the upside of Micron in isolation.
