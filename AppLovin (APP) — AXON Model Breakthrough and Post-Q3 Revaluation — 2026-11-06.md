# AppLovin after Q3 2026 — why AXON works, why shares rose, and what is now priced in

**Analysis date:** November 6, 2026 (Europe/Berlin)  
**Price anchor:** $356.10, **November 5 regular US closing price**, not a live November 6 fill. Previous close $271.75; next-session move **+31.04%**.  
**Status:** Research update; existing **$10,000 virtual APP position remains unchanged**. No sale or purchase instruction.

## Executive conclusion

AppLovin's strength is not generic "AI hype." It combines (a) predictive auction optimization (AXON), (b) access to mobile ad supply through publisher and mediation relationships including MAX, (c) measurable advertiser return on ad spend (ROAS), (d) potentially reinforcing performance signals and a low marginal cost of monetization, (e) consumer/e-commerce expansion, and (f) substantial free cash flow and share repurchases.

Q3 provided **real evidence** that a partially deployed new model architecture increases profitable advertiser spend, while serving expense as a fraction of revenue declined. Yet the 31% stock jump priced in considerably more than just one quarter's $100m+ revenue surprise: it is also a re-rating of durability, future model improvements, consumer-market expansion, and reduced fear about an earlier slowdown. Its market cap rose by approximately **$28 billion** at a roughly constant 331m shares (271.75 to 356.10); this is directional, not an exact market-cap reconciliation.

At ~$356, the share price is higher and the old $228.50-purchase-date model cannot be reused as a current return estimate. APP is **not directly exposed to bank financing of hyperscale datacenter projects** in the same way as Oracle or CoreWeave. It has real model/competition/platform risks and volatility. The current 2027 forward multiple is **~18.1x if illustrative 2027 EPS of $19.63 materializes**; it is not automatically excessive for a company growing at current rates. However, strong alpha beyond the market now requires still-strong multiyear EPS growth and/or retention of a healthy future P/E.

## 1. How AppLovin makes money

A seller of advertising (app publisher) has an impression to sell. Advertisers seek purchases, installs and users with positive unit economics. AXON predicts which impression is worth what to which campaign given estimated conversion and future customer value, and its bidding/auction systems allocate traffic. Performance that meets advertiser target ROAS encourages advertisers to bid and spend more. More demand can also increase inventory monetization for publisher partners. MAX mediation and other platform services strengthen access to supply; Axon Ads Manager makes campaigns more accessible. This is not a guarantee of a moat: data access and policy rules are partly controlled by third-party app platforms, and Unity, Meta, Google and others can compete.

A potential flywheel is more useful ads -> higher advertiser return -> higher budgets and network activity -> better signals -> improving model -> better results, but **we cannot currently quantify incremental causal lift versus selection, pricing and broader demand** from external disclosures. Likewise, do not confuse gross advertiser spend with AppLovin net reported revenue.

AppLovin exited the more capital- and operations-intensive app ownership business in 2025 to concentrate on high-margin advertising software, supporting an asset-light model.

## 2. Why the 5 November price move happened

1. Q3 reported revenue **$2.185bn (+55% YoY)**; net income **$1.439bn (+72%)**; diluted GAAP EPS **$4.30**; Adjusted EBITDA **$1.842bn at 84% margin**; FCF approximately **$1.418bn**. Revenue beat market estimates around **$2.07–2.08bn**, EPS exceeded approximately **$4.03** expected.
2. Core mobile gaming advertising reaccelerated to **nearly 50% YoY growth**, management chiefly attributing Q3 sequential growth of 14% to new model improvements.
3. **A new model generation was only partially rolled out.** Management says the architecture improves return on ad spend, especially when training signals are sparse (new/smaller advertisers and newer consumer categories). This creates an option for further growth; size, durability and full deployment dates are unconfirmed.
4. Consumer advertisers' spend grew **triple digits YoY and double digits sequentially**, with an undisclosed absolute base. This is encouraging but cannot yet prove consumer alone will dominate economics.
5. Q4 guidance **$2.365–2.395bn revenue** and **$1.995–2.025bn Adjusted EBITDA** points to continuing sequential growth. Midpoint ~$2.38bn versus approximately $2.25bn consensus preprint.
6. Despite extra model-training compute, datacenter cost declined **as percent of revenue**; increased revenue did not destroy cash margins. Q2 FCF suffered cash-tax timing, so the Q3 rebound should not be naively annualized.
7. Announced total current authorization for buybacks **$3.6bn**; Q3 reported **$1.4bn in repurchases AND tax withholding**, not all independent open-market buybacks.
8. Crucially, the stock had suffered a huge preceding derating. The price reacted to a change in expectations, not merely another good quarter.

Company Q3 balance sheet: **$3.174bn cash and ~$3.516bn long-term debt**; net debt ~$0.34bn. Low net leverage reduces immediate credit-funding risk, though gross debt remains.

## 3. Is the stock now too expensive?

Snapshot at $356.10: ~331m shares, roughly **$118bn market cap** and **~23.9x trailing GAAP EPS $14.91**. For FY2026, Q1–Q3 reported revenue $5.951bn plus Q4 midpoint $2.380bn equals ~$8.331bn projected full-year revenue. Price/full-year-revenue therefore approx 14.2x. A secondary-data analyst forecast points to **2027 EPS ~$19.63**, implying **18.1x** 2027 EPS. Forecast definitions and reporting timing differ among sources; use an EPS-consistent valuation framework for live investing.

A 31% price increase while the latest quarterly EPS beat was only ~7% and revenue beat ~5% means the stock re-rated expectations of future performance as well as discounting reported Q3 numbers. A 31% increase in fair value would require either durable higher forecast profits, a lower risk discount / higher justified multiple, or both. Do not infer that a rally must mean overvaluation.

The **original November 2 purchase memo** used $228.50 and a middle-case **2030 price ~$371**, implying roughly +4pp annual alpha versus a 9% assumed benchmark. The identical 2030 endpoint from $356.10 would now imply only **~4% cumulative price appreciation**. The old endpoint *must be revised if new fundamental evidence justifies it*, rather than mechanically preserving the old alpha label.

## 5. What precisely makes further high returns plausible?

Three independent earnings-growth pathways should be tested separately: **(A)** improving gaming ROAS leads to an enduring budget share shift; **(B)** consumer/e-commerce budgets scale without sacrificing ROAS and despite fourth-quarter seasonality; **(C)** a larger profit base plus buybacks grows EPS per share while serving costs remain modest. Management discusses roughly **30% long-term business compounding**, but this is its ambition, not an independently demonstrated 2028–30 CAGR or EPS forecast.

The biggest uncertainty is whether AppLovin's model lead is *durable and economically defensible*. A short model advantage can be copied, platform policies can shift, rival ad auctions can improve, and customers can migrate; growth can slow in advertising cycles. The data/reach flywheel is a hypothesis to measure, not a verified monopolistic guarantee.

## 6. Monitoring and decision rules

- Each quarter: gaming advertising growth, consumer advertiser spend (ideally absolute), ROAS improvements validated beyond management claims, model rollout progress and A/B evidence where disclosed.
- Revenue and EPS vs previous guidance / pre-quarter market consensus; **check sustainable 2028–30 estimates**, not one-quarter beats.
- FCF/GAAP net income and normalized FCF conversion over 4 quarters; SBC, share count and actual cash spent on buybacks.
- Platform policy and measurement/attribution restrictions; competitive performance of Unity Vector, Meta and Google.
- Sector sensitivity: advertising cyclicality (distinct from datacenter financing risk).
- Recompute expected relative alpha using the current share price. If the expected return falls below the index despite credible optimistic growth, consider trim; if model advantage persists while price falls, valuation can improve.
- Keep APP virtual holding at **$10,000 nominal** until an explicit separate trade instruction. No sale implied by this note.
