**Company:** Wolters Kluwer N.V.

**Action:** Virtual Buy  
**Virtual allocation:** **USD 10,000**  
**Reference portfolio:** **USD 1,000,000**  
**Portfolio weight:** **1.0%**  
**Position role:** Initial position in a high-quality, low-valuation professional information / workflow business facing a real but potentially over-discounted AI disruption risk.

---

## Why I am investing

I am investing **USD 10,000 in Wolters Kluwer**, equal to 1% of my USD 1 million reference portfolio.

The main reason is not that I think Wolters Kluwer will become a dominant AI company. The reason is simpler: **the market now values the company as though AI will permanently damage a meaningful part of its business model, while that damage is still barely visible in the current operating results.**

Wolters Kluwer is still growing organically in the mid-single digits, has a high share of recurring revenue, strong margins, ROIC around 18–19%, and generates roughly EUR 1.3 billion of free cash flow. At the same time, the shares trade at roughly 12x forward earnings.

For a business with those current economics, that valuation is low.

I therefore do not need a perfect outcome for the investment to work. The stock can produce a reasonable return if Wolters Kluwer simply preserves most of its current economic core, continues to grow moderately, and AI destroys less pricing power than the market currently fears.

---

## Why AI does not have to be a pure threat

Wolters Kluwer is not only exposed to AI disruption from the outside. It is already integrating AI into products such as UpToDate, CCH Axcess and Libra.

The important part of the business is not just information retrieval. Wolters Kluwer also sits inside professional workflows where trust, structured domain knowledge, compliance, provenance, state and execution matter.

If the company can continue moving from:

**Search → Decision → Workflow → Action**

then there is additional upside beyond the current business.

I am not paying for that outcome in the base case. I treat it as optionality.

---

## Why I am not making the position larger

The long-term risk is real, and it is broader than simple product replacement.

Wolters Kluwer can lose economics in at least three ways:

1. **Product replacement** — foundation models or AI-native products replace parts of the current offering.
2. **Monetization erosion** — AI increases customer productivity and reduces seat counts faster than Wolters Kluwer can shift pricing toward value, usage, workflow, transactions or premium AI.
3. **Interface loss** — Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic or another agent owns the customer interface while Wolters Kluwer is pushed toward a lower-value data or API role in the background.

This means Wolters Kluwer can remain technically useful and still capture less economic value.

That is the main reason I am not starting with a larger position.

---

## Portfolio correlation matters

I already own SAP, Salesforce, UiPath and Pegasystems, which have partial exposure to the same broader thesis: AI may commoditize outer software and information layers faster than it destroys the institutional state, workflow, trust and execution layers underneath them.

Wolters Kluwer is not the same company and does not have the same business model, but the key failure mode is partially correlated.

If foundation models or external agents move faster than expected into workflow state and execution, several of these positions could be damaged at the same time.

So this is not a fully independent bet.

That hidden thesis correlation lowers the appropriate position size.

---

## Why USD 10,000 is the right starting size

USD 10,000 is large enough for the position to matter if the market has overreacted to AI disruption risk, but small enough that I am not overexposed to a still-unresolved technological transition.

The current valuation provides meaningful downside protection relative to the quality of the existing business, but the expected return is not so far above my required hurdle that I want to treat the stock as a no-brainer.

I would consider increasing the position later if either:

- the share price falls further while the operating thesis remains intact, or
- the company provides stronger evidence that AI is improving pricing, retention, ARPU, cross-sell or new ARR without losing the customer interface.

I would be much more cautious if Wolters Kluwer remains relevant technically but begins losing economic capture through lower seat counts, weaker pricing or dependence on third-party agents.

---

## What would make me add

Potential positive evidence includes:

- recurring organic growth remaining around the current mid-to-high single-digit range,
- continued strong cloud growth,
- stable or improving pricing and retention,
- Expert AI products creating measurable incremental ARR or ARPU,
- evidence that seat erosion is being offset by value-, usage-, transaction- or workflow-based pricing,
- WKL retaining the primary workflow interface rather than becoming only a backend data provider,
- stronger cross-product reuse of AI infrastructure,
- margins and ROIC remaining high despite continued AI investment.

---

## What would weaken or kill the thesis

Major negative evidence includes:

- total organic growth falling sustainably below roughly 3%,
- recurring revenue growth falling below roughly 4% for an extended period,
- recurring cloud growth falling structurally below roughly 8%,
- ROIC falling below roughly 15% without a clear temporary explanation,
- adjusted free cash flow falling structurally below roughly EUR 1.2 billion while revenue is still growing,
- AI products achieving high usage but failing to produce better pricing, retention, ARPU, cross-sell or new ARR,
- seat erosion reducing revenue faster than new pricing models can compensate,
- third-party AI agents becoming the dominant professional interface and forcing WKL toward lower-value data/API monetization,
- AI-native competitors taking over not only search but also workflow state and execution,
- WKL needing permanently high acquisition spending simply to maintain mid-single-digit growth.

---

## Decision summary

I am not buying Wolters Kluwer because I am sure AI will make the company stronger.

I am buying because the current price already assumes a meaningful amount of long-term damage, while the existing business has not yet shown that damage in its operating results.

The company still has strong recurring revenue, high returns on capital, strong cash generation and deep professional workflows. That gives me a real business underneath the AI debate rather than a purely speculative future platform.

At the same time, the unresolved risks around product replacement, monetization erosion and interface loss are large enough that I do not want to make this a major position immediately.

**USD 10,000 / 1.0% is therefore the appropriate starting allocation.**

The position gives me exposure to a potentially significant expectation gap while keeping the cost of being wrong on the long-term AI transition manageable.
