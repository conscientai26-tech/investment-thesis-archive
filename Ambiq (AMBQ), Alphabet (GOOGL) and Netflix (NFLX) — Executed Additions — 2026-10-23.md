## Why I bought during the decline

Prices have been falling, including in companies I already own. That was the occasion to review the positions and put more capital to work. It was not the investment thesis.

I have a rule in my [investment framework](https://app.notion.com/p/40167b6787f8811d960ad27ab4541cd2): **a falling stock is not automatically a buy, and a rising stock is not automatically a warning.** The direction of the last price move does not tell me whether the next several years will be good for shareholders. I care about the business, the price I am paying for its future cash flows, and what the market is already expecting.

This is also the point of my [Expectation Gap Principle](https://app.notion.com/p/3d167b6787f8815a8924c0a9add9af29). A lower price matters when expectations have fallen more than the underlying prospects justify. It does not matter simply because a stock used to trade higher. I cannot prove from a price chart alone that these three stocks are undervalued. The additional purchases express my judgment that the prospective returns have become more attractive relative to the risks and the alternatives I have examined.

I am not making a one-day call on the market, and I am not claiming to have identified the bottom. A continued decline would not, by itself, invalidate the purchases. A recovery tomorrow would not, by itself, validate them.

## Ambiq Micro — added $1,500

My reason for owning Ambiq goes back to the Interconnected AI thesis I began documenting in August: personal AI is likely to spread across devices rather than live solely in one large cloud model. Watches, glasses, sensors and other small devices cannot run power-hungry computation continuously. Useful on-device intelligence will depend in part on processing more information using very little energy.

Ambiq is a relatively direct, concentrated way of expressing that view. I like its exposure to ultra-low-power computing, but the path from a promising technical position to durable earnings is not guaranteed. Strong revenue growth does not settle questions about competitive pressure, customer concentration, margins or eventual free cash flow. Open hardware and AI tooling could expand the market and also weaken individual suppliers' pricing power.

For that reason I added **$1,500**, not the larger amount considered in the earlier October 22 proposal. The nominal position is now **$6,500**. I wanted more exposure without letting the most speculative of these three investments dominate the additions.

## Alphabet — added $1,000

Alphabet is a different part of the same structural bet. Its advantage is not limited to one model or one piece of hardware. It already has Android, distribution through widely used products, cloud infrastructure and Gemini. If personal AI becomes a system linking devices, information and actions, those existing assets could become more valuable.

There is a real cost to that opportunity. AI infrastructure requires enormous investment, and spending is not equivalent to returns. The question is whether the additional usage and economic power translate into enough incremental cash flow per share. I still consider Alphabet one of the stronger businesses through which to hold this thesis, so I added **$1,000**, taking the nominal allocation to **$12,000**.

## Netflix — added $1,500

Netflix is not an Interconnected AI investment. I added to it for a separate reason: the gap that may be opening between a falling share price and the earning power of an established business.

My [October 22 review](https://app.notion.com/p/40167b6787f881249d54d61bb85c463c) found a much more profitable Netflix than the one investors worried about in 2022, alongside genuine concerns about slower revenue growth, advertising execution and competition for attention. It also found that the reported 2026 free cash flow includes a one-time payment and must not be treated as fully recurring. That qualification matters.

The stock's decline has made the valuation worth revisiting. My working view is that the market may be discounting Netflix's ability to keep generating and growing cash flow per share too heavily. That is a judgment, not a result already demonstrated by the numbers. If advertising disappointments are structural, content economics weaken or engagement loses ground, the market's caution could be justified.

I increased the nominal Netflix allocation from **$6,000 to $7,500**. I am not averaging down to repair the appearance of a loss. The previous entry price is irrelevant to the expected return from this point onward.

## What changed, and what did not

The three additions total **$4,000**. An earlier October 22 proposal considered larger additions to Ambiq and Alphabet and a new Qualcomm position. This record supersedes that *proposal for these specific additions*; it does **not** claim a Qualcomm purchase was executed. I deliberately spread this smaller amount across two parts of the AI thesis and one separate cash-generating business.

I still need to track whether the hypotheses are being borne out. For Ambiq, that means customer diversification, defensible low-power technology, growth quality and a credible path to cash generation. For Alphabet, it means the return on incremental AI spending and value captured through its products and platforms. For Netflix, it means recurring free cash flow, advertising monetization, retention, content spending and growth per share after buybacks.

The useful question is not whether the next price tick is green. It is whether these businesses are worth more, over time, than the future their prices currently imply.
