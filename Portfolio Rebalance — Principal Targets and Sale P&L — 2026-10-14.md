## Two companies I want to check again

**Meta.** The stock has moved a lot since I first bought it. I want to know why. Did advertising earnings and cash-flow expectations improve enough to explain the move? How much is AI monetization, how much is a higher valuation multiple, and how much incremental capital will the company have to spend? I am keeping the $10,000 principal target while I do that work. A higher price alone is not a reason to sell.

**Wolters Kluwer.** I also want to understand the recent rally properly before treating its old valuation gap as still available. Was the rise caused by better operating evidence, less fear of AI disruption, or just a rerating? I need to compare the current price with normalized owner cash flow, the pricing power of its actual workflow products and the damage the market is still assuming. The principal target is $9,000 for now. I am not marking the research as complete.

## What would actually be locked in by the sales?

There is a difference between deciding to reduce my **original invested capital** and recording a **realized profit**. A realized profit exists only when the shares are sold. No brokerage or paper-trading executions have been verified for the new reductions, so the amount **confirmed as realized from this rebalance is $0**.

## Why I'm doing this

I am investing for five to ten years. I want more of my own capital in a few businesses I believe can keep compounding, without letting any one unresolved thesis dominate the portfolio. That is why I am adding principal to Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon while taking back part of my UiPath, Salesforce, SAP and Wolters Kluwer allocations.

Meta, Wolters Kluwer and the other holdings are not automatically better or worse investments because their prices recently moved. I want to understand what changed in the companies, what changed in market expectations and which of those changes is likely to matter over the next five to ten years.
