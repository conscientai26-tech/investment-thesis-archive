## Why Tokyo Ohka

My original reason for owning the company still stands. Advanced semiconductor manufacturing needs specialized materials, not just more chips, GPUs and fabs. Tokyo Ohka supplies photoresists and high-purity process chemicals used in manufacturing. These products have to meet demanding yield, purity and consistency requirements. A customer cannot change a qualified material as casually as switching a generic supplier.

The attraction is not that Tokyo Ohka has a monopoly. It does not. It is that qualification, process know-how and established customer relationships can create real switching costs in a market where a small material failure can be extremely expensive.

The first half of 2026 provided operating evidence for the thesis. In the prior research entry I documented first-half revenue of ¥139.668 billion, up 25.1%, and operating profit of ¥28.854 billion, up 45.4%. Demand for advanced resist materials and high-purity chemicals had been growing. Management also raised its full-year outlook during the summer. Those figures matter more to me than the mere fact that the share price has bounced.

What I am buying is exposure to a company that may benefit from the increasing complexity and volume of semiconductor fabrication, especially if it can turn its new capacity into attractive cash returns.

## The price still matters

Tokyo Ohka closed on November 9 at **¥6,687**, up 4.42% from the previous close of ¥6,404. That is a substantial rebound, but the stock is still below my earlier October 27 reference of ¥7,750.

Using the previously published FY2026 earnings estimate of about ¥355 per share as a fixed reference, ¥6,687 would imply roughly **18.8× projected earnings**. This is an illustrative multiple, not a freshly verified consensus estimate or a guarantee that the forecast survives tomorrow's report.

I am not buying because the stock is below its former high. A drawdown by itself says nothing about value. I am buying because the decline has produced a price at which the existing operating thesis still looks worth underwriting, even after today's bounce. I think there is a credible possibility that the market has marked down the business more severely than its fundamentals ultimately warrant.

That possibility is enough for me to take a position. It is not enough to claim that tomorrow's earnings will beat expectations.

## Why I am buying *before* the report

Waiting until after earnings would give me more information. It could also mean entering at a very different price if the results remove some of the uncertainty currently depressing the valuation.

I am choosing exposure before that information arrives. This is a deliberate risk, not a claim of foresight. A strong quarter could still lead to a falling share price if expectations were higher. A weak outlook or disappointing cash conversion could make today's valuation look expensive.

If the shares gap down, I will have to assess whether the report damages the long-term thesis or merely changes sentiment. I will not assume a lower price automatically means a better bargain.

## The part that could prove me wrong

Capital intensity is the central weakness I need to watch. Photoresist research, purification, manufacturing facilities and working capital are expensive. Rising revenue and accounting earnings are not enough if expansion absorbs the cash that shareholders expect to receive.

I want to see whether advanced-material demand persists, whether margins hold, whether guidance changes, and whether new plants can reach productive utilization. I also need to watch competitive pressure, customer qualification losses and the possibility that the semiconductor investment cycle slows.

There is portfolio risk here too. My other holdings include several businesses linked to the same AI and semiconductor spending cycle. Tokyo Ohka has a different role in that supply chain, but it is not an independent hedge against a sector-wide correction.

At the last documented model-capital reference of approximately $142,848, a $17,000 position represents **about 11.9%** of the portfolio. A 20% drawdown in this position alone would cost approximately $3,400, or 2.38 percentage points of that capital base, before currency movements. I accept that concentration knowingly.

## What will count as a successful decision

Not one green candle.

I want evidence that the company can sustain advanced-material growth, defend its position at critical process steps and eventually turn capital expenditure into higher free cash flow. The earnings reaction will be useful information, but it will not be my only measure of the investment.

If the numbers disprove the assumptions behind the valuation, I will change my assessment. If the numbers support them, a volatile share price will not by itself change my view.
