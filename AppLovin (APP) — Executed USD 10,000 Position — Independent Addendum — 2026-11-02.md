## Why the purchase was completed

I assigned **$10,000** to AppLovin because the relationship between current earnings power, market valuation, and plausible long-run earnings persistence appeared favorable enough to warrant a material portfolio position. The decision is **not conditional** on the next earnings release or on obtaining a better price. The position is recorded as bought.

The investment case is based on the following causal mechanism:

1. AXON's advertising optimization attempts to improve the economic yield of each purchased impression. If its predictions are measurably better than competing alternatives, advertisers may earn more conversion value per dollar of spending.
2. Sustained advertiser returns can support retention and greater budgets. Higher platform activity can, subject to data access and feedback quality, reinforce optimization.
3. Additional revenue may have high incremental margins because the platform does not need to increase its cost base proportionally with every incremental dollar of advertising activity.
4. Earnings and free cash flow can compound per share if the competitive advantage persists and the diluted share count is controlled.
5. The share price need not capitalize the company's recent 50%-plus revenue growth indefinitely for a multi-year investment to beat a reasonable benchmark.

The underlying claim is economic, not semantic: that differentiated allocation technology can produce observable advertiser utility, monetizable revenue, and cash available to shareholders. Terms such as 'AI platform' alone do not constitute evidence of a moat.

In particular, quarterly net income and free cash flow are not equal.

## Why $10,000 rather than $7,000 or $12,500

**$7,000 was an explicitly stated minimum, not the desired exact allocation.** I selected **$10,000** because the modeled upside warrants a consequential position, while the reliability of the moat and the company-specific downside are less certain than in the portfolio's most diversified businesses.

Historical position sizes used for relative calibration included approximately $12,500 in SAP, Salesforce and UiPath; $11,000 in Synopsys; $10,000 in Meta and Wolters Kluwer; $9,000 in Cadence; and smaller initial amounts in Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft. Those historical figures are **not** a statement of current live holdings: subsequent trimming, sales, and new positions changed the portfolio.

AppLovin carries materially higher **idiosyncratic risk** than Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Synopsys, or Wolters Kluwer. Against Micron and SK Hynix the risk is comparably substantial but different: memory-price and capex cycles versus platform access, advertising model durability, and regulatory dependence. UiPath and Pegasystems cannot be ranked on a single objective risk axis without estimating comparable distributions of permanent loss.

At a $10,000 allocation, declines of 30%, 50%, and 70% translate to **$3,000, $5,000, and $7,000** of loss, respectively. These are mechanical stress tests, not probabilities or maximum-loss bounds. A full loss of the allocated principal remains mathematically possible.

I did not authorize a larger $12,500 position solely to maximize nominal alpha. Position size should reflect uncertainty in the thesis, not just the point estimate of its upside. I also do not claim an exact percentage of the current whole portfolio without reconciling the latest purchases, sales, cash and FX.

## What could invalidate the decision

**Business economics:** Sustained evidence of worse advertiser outcomes or shrinking relative AXON performance; weakening advertiser retention or budget growth; a structurally lower ability to convert revenue into free cash flow.

**External platform control:** Adverse changes in Apple, Google or Meta distribution, data, attribution or privacy policies could weaken the business even if AppLovin's engineering execution remained strong.

**Legal and regulatory exposure:** Litigation and reported regulatory scrutiny remain real uncertainties but not proof of wrongdoing. The adverse and no-adverse-outcome paths must be distinguished and revised when evidence changes.

**Valuation and expectations:** Even a growing company can materially underperform when investors lower both the forward earnings estimate and the acceptable multiple. A severe downside scenario is not a floor.

**Ownership and forced distribution:** Q2 2026 institutional 13F aggregations showed major increases among some holders, including Invesco, Capital World Investors and FMR, while others reported reductions. Those were dated quarter-end snapshots, **not** observed November 2 buying or selling. Material coordinated position reductions by large funds can increase price pressure and concentration risk, but are not automatically evidence of superior information. Q3 filings and holder-by-holder share changes will be examined when available.

**Portfolio correlation:** Exposure to advertising demand and platform regulation overlaps with Meta and Alphabet. AI-related sentiment repricing can affect several portfolio holdings at once despite different business models.
