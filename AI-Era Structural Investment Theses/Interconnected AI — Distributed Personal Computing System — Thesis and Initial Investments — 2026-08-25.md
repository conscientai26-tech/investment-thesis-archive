# 1. Core Thesis

The next major step in artificial intelligence may not require another fundamental leap in raw model intelligence.

A large part of the necessary intelligence already exists.

Modern AI systems can understand language, interpret images and audio, use tools, interact with software, maintain context, create plans, and increasingly execute multi-step tasks.

The next structural transition may therefore be less:

> **AI becomes dramatically more intelligent.**

and more:

> **Existing AI intelligence becomes organized across devices, applications, sensors, services and physical systems.**

Instead of isolated intelligent products, a coordinated intelligent system begins to emerge.

Today, smartphones, watches, earbuds, glasses, cars, smart homes, calendars, email systems, navigation tools and AI assistants still largely behave as separate products.

The longer-term transition is toward a **distributed personal computing system** in which specialized nodes contribute perception, context, computation, communication and physical action to a common evolving AI state.

This is a shift from **device-centric computing** toward **state-centric computing**.

---

# 2. From Device-Centric to State-Centric Computing

Current computing is predominantly device-centric.

The user decides:

> I need navigation → open Maps.

> I need communication → open WhatsApp.

> I need information → open Search.

> I want to control the house → open a smart-home application.

A state-centric architecture works differently.

The system continuously maintains relevant state:

- where the user is,
- what the user is doing,
- what is scheduled,
- what happened previously,
- which people are relevant,
- which devices are available,
- what those devices currently observe,
- what actions they can perform,
- what permissions apply,
- what the user is trying to accomplish.

Devices then become different interfaces into the same evolving state.

The phone, watch, glasses, car and home are no longer simply five independent computers.

They become specialized nodes inside **one persistent personal AI system**.

The center of gravity moves away from the individual device and toward the shared state that survives across devices.

# 3. Specialized Nodes

Different hardware categories naturally occupy different roles.

## Smartphone

General compute, connectivity, communications, permissions, apps, authentication and display.

## Smartwatch / Wrist Interface

Body state, movement, health signals, identity, persistent sensing, quick confirmations and potentially silent physical input.

## Earbuds

Persistent voice input and private audio output.

## Smart Glasses

Visual perception, immediate environmental context and potentially heads-up output.

## Car

Mobility, location, cameras, sensors, compute and physical actuation.

## Smart Home

Environmental state and a large number of controllable physical devices.

## Cameras and Sensors

Persistent environmental perception.

## Robots

General-purpose physical actuation.

The importance of each category changes once AI can coordinate them.

A watch is no longer merely a small screen.

A pair of glasses is no longer merely a camera.

A car is no longer merely transportation.

A home appliance is no longer merely an isolated appliance.

Each becomes a node inside a broader intelligence network.

# 4. Why This Does Not Require Superintelligence

This transition does not require superintelligence.

The critical technologies already exist in early but usable forms:

- multimodal perception,
- tool use,
- agents,
- memory,
- cloud and edge compute,
- connected devices,
- smart-home APIs,
- wearables,
- increasingly capable vehicles,
- cross-application actions.

The difficult missing layer is increasingly:

# Orchestration Infrastructure

The system must decide:

- which information matters,
- which sensor should provide it,
- which device has authority,
- which application should be used,
- which model should perform the reasoning,
- whether computation should happen locally or in the cloud,
- what permissions apply,
- which device should execute the final action,
- how the result updates persistent state.

This is primarily an infrastructure, reliability, permissions, state-management and coordination problem.

It is not dependent on a hypothetical future superintelligence.

That means very clear forms of interconnected AI can emerge on a relatively short technological horizon.

# 5. A Simple Example

Suppose the user has an important meeting.

**Calendar:** meeting at 09:30.

**Email:** location changed.

**Maps:** traffic is unusually heavy.

**Watch:** user woke up late.

**Home:** user is still inside the house.

**Car:** charged and available.

**Earbuds:** available for private communication.

An orchestrated system can infer:

> The user needs to leave twelve minutes earlier.

It could then:

- notify the user,
- prepare navigation,
- precondition the car,
- summarize the relevant message,
- prepare the house for absence,
- prepare a message to other participants if necessary.

None of these actions individually represents a technological revolution.

**The revolution is that they form one coherent action chain.**

# 6. Combinatorial Utility

The utility of interconnected devices may be greater than the simple sum of their individual utilities.

In a mostly isolated system:

\[
U \approx \sum U_i
\]

In an interconnected system:

\[
U =
\sum U_i
+
\sum Interactions_{ij}
+
Context
+
Orchestration
+
Automation
\]

A watch knowing the user's physiological state has limited value.

Glasses understanding the immediate environment have limited value.

Calendar understanding commitments has limited value.

Maps understanding transport conditions has limited value.

But their combination can produce decisions that none could produce independently.

This creates a potentially important economic effect:

> **The marginal value of adding another useful node can rise as the ecosystem becomes denser.**

That is very different from the normal hardware model in which every additional product is evaluated largely on its standalone specifications.

# 7. The Ecosystem Flywheel

A manufacturer with many interconnected nodes can potentially generate a positive feedback loop:

**more devices**

→ more context

→ better coordination

→ higher utility

→ stronger incentive to buy another compatible device

→ still more context and control.

This can increase:

- retention,
- switching costs,
- devices per user,
- average selling prices,
- service revenue,
- recurring revenue,
- ecosystem loyalty.

The important shift is therefore not merely:

> more connected gadgets.

It is:

> **connected hardware becoming a platform asset.**

This is especially important for companies that are still valued primarily as hardware manufacturers.

If AI changes the economic quality of the ecosystem, the market may eventually have to change the category through which it values the company.

# 8. The Thesis Does Not Require Any One Company to Win

This is a critical clarification.

The thesis does **not** claim that Alphabet, Meta, Xiaomi, Apple, Ambiq or any other specific company will become the dominant winner of Interconnected AI.

It makes a weaker and more robust prediction:

> **If interconnected AI becomes a normal part of computing, companies that already control valuable layers of the emerging architecture can benefit even if they do not dominate the entire stack.**

This distinction matters enormously.

Xiaomi does not need to defeat Google.

Meta does not need to defeat Apple.

Google does not need to own every physical device.

Ambiq does not need to become the dominant semiconductor company.

A company can benefit because the technological transition makes **its existing assets more valuable**.

For example:

- Google may benefit because AI increases the value of Gmail, Calendar, Maps, Android, Search and Gemini as a digital context layer.
- Xiaomi may benefit because AI increases the usefulness and lock-in of its physical device ecosystem.
- Meta may benefit because glasses, messaging, social context and human-facing interfaces become more important.
- Apple may benefit because vertical integration makes its existing ecosystem substantially more useful.
- Ambiq may benefit because a large increase in always-on AI nodes raises demand for energy-efficient local computation.

These companies can all benefit simultaneously, even if only one or none of them ultimately controls the central orchestration layer.

The investment thesis is therefore not:

> These are the companies that will win.

It is:

> **These companies own assets whose economic value may increase disproportionately if the computing architecture moves in the predicted direction.**

Mathematically, the requirement is not:

\[
ValueCapture_{Xiaomi} > ValueCapture_{Google}
\]

It is only that the shift creates enough incremental economic value for the security to be mispriced:

\[
\Delta Value_{Company} > ValueAlreadyPricedIn
\]

The question is therefore one of **incremental value capture relative to expectations**, not absolute technological dominance.

# 9. Competitive Complementarity

The architecture does not require Big Tech to cooperate harmoniously.

The more realistic model is:

# Competitive Complementarity

Companies can simultaneously:

- integrate with each other,
- depend on each other,
- compete for adjacent layers,
- attempt to internalize strategically important functions.

Google can want Gemini on Xiaomi hardware while simultaneously trying to control more orchestration.

Xiaomi can integrate Gemini internationally while building its own AI and orchestration technologies.

Meta can integrate third-party services into glasses while trying to make Meta AI the user's primary assistant.

Apple can integrate external models while protecting its control over hardware, permissions, user state and operating systems.

This already resembles the existing technology industry.

Google earns substantial economic value on Apple devices despite competing with Apple.

Microsoft and Google compete while their products interoperate.

AWS hosts companies that compete with Amazon.

Android manufacturers depend on Google while simultaneously building proprietary ecosystem layers.

Interconnected AI can therefore become a market of **overlapping strategic domains**, rather than either perfect cooperation or a single monopoly.

# 10. Hierarchical Orchestration

There may be no single universal orchestrator.

Instead, orchestration can be hierarchical.

A high-level agent can decide the goal while lower-level domain systems determine how to execute it.

For example:

> Prepare the home for my arrival.

A high-level digital agent does not need to know every low-level protocol used by every air conditioner, light, lock and robot.

It can issue a goal to the home orchestration layer.

The local ecosystem can then decide:

- temperature,
- lighting,
- appliance state,
- security state,
- robot state.

A plausible architecture may therefore include:

### Google
Digital orchestration.

### Xiaomi
Physical home / car / device orchestration.

### Meta
Human / social / interface orchestration.

### Apple
Integrated personal orchestration inside its own ecosystem.

A higher-level goal can pass between these domains.

This reduces the need for one company to understand every low-level device protocol and creates multiple economic control points.

# 18. Interface Friction, Liability and Security

Cross-company orchestration creates genuine problems.

If one company's AI interprets an intention, another company's operating system executes it through a car, and a third company's glasses provide visual confirmation, who is responsible when something fails?

Important constraints include:

- permissions,
- security,
- data ownership,
- privacy,
- latency,
- API reliability,
- financial liability,
- physical safety,
- regulatory responsibility.

These factors create an advantage for vertically integrated systems such as Apple.

They also strengthen the case for hierarchical orchestration, where each company retains responsibility for a bounded execution domain.

For example, a general agent can decide that the home should enter an arrival state, while Xiaomi itself remains responsible for deciding how Xiaomi devices perform that task.

This can reduce cross-company liability and implementation complexity.

The existence of interface friction does not invalidate the thesis.

It changes the architecture through which the thesis is likely to materialize.

One of the deeper assumptions behind the thesis is that model intelligence may become less scarce over time.

If models improve while the cost of inference falls and high-quality intelligence becomes available from multiple providers, the economically scarce assets can migrate elsewhere.

Possible scarce assets include:

- distribution,
- identity,
- context,
- permissions,
- sensor access,
- device fleets,
- physical actuators,
- user relationships,
- persistent state,
- orchestration infrastructure,
- energy-efficient local compute.

This can strengthen companies that do not own the best model.

Xiaomi does not necessarily need to beat Gemini.

It can potentially use external intelligence while retaining valuable control over:

> HyperOS + device graph + device state + permissions + physical execution.

The dangerous opposite scenario for Xiaomi is:

> Google owns intelligence + memory + context + orchestration, while Xiaomi devices become interchangeable commodity actuators.

That is a central Xiaomi kill condition and one of the reasons the position is large relative to earlier pilot investments but not unlimited.

# 20. Ambiq and the Energy Bottleneck

Interconnected AI creates another important infrastructure requirement:

**always-on intelligence must consume extremely little energy.**

Glasses, watches, earbuds, sensors and similar products have severe constraints on:

- battery size,
- heat,
- weight,
- physical dimensions.

Yet they increasingly need to:

- monitor sensors,
- process audio,
- classify visual information,
- detect relevant events,
- perform local inference,
- decide when to wake larger processors or cloud AI.

This makes:

> **useful compute per joule**

strategically valuable.

Ambiq specializes in ultra-low-power compute.

Its potential role is not necessarily to run the largest models.

Instead:

> keep the distributed AI nervous system awake efficiently.

A possible architecture is:

Sensors run continuously
→ ultra-low-power chip processes them
→ relevant event detected
→ larger Qualcomm / Google / Apple / cloud AI is activated.

Ambiq therefore does not need to build the world's strongest AI processor.

It needs to answer:

> How much useful intelligence can run continuously using as little power as possible?

If interconnected AI creates hundreds of millions or billions of additional always-on nodes, Ambiq and similar providers can benefit even if completely different companies dominate the consumer platforms.

This is why Ambiq is structurally attractive as a manufacturer-independent infrastructure exposure.

# 24. Why the Market Can See the Trend and Still Misprice It

The prediction:

> AI will become interconnected

is not by itself enough to generate alpha.

The market may understand:

> Watches and glasses get AI.

while underestimating:

- how much more valuable devices become together,
- how much ecosystem lock-in increases,
- how valuable persistent context becomes,
- how quickly orchestration becomes useful,
- how important physical device graphs become,
- how rapidly model intelligence commoditizes,
- which infrastructure bottlenecks emerge,
- how much several different companies can benefit simultaneously.

The mispricing can therefore occur one or two causal steps beyond the obvious technological prediction.

The important chain is:

**technological transition**
→ **new behavior**
→ **new economic value**
→ **specific asset becomes more valuable**
→ **company captures part of that value**
→ **market had not already priced enough of it**.

Xiaomi has limited U.S. relevance and remains exposed to regulatory and geopolitical shocks.

## AI Execution Risk

Xiaomi may fail to build enough internal AI and orchestration capability to retain strategic value above the device layer.

## Fragmentation

The device ecosystem can remain technically connected but too fragmented to create meaningful cross-device utility.

## Evidence That Would Strengthen the Position

- clear growth in devices per user,
- stronger cross-device automation,
- evidence that HyperOS retains meaningful state and permissions,
- increasing Internet / service monetization,
- EV economics improving,
- AI features materially increasing retention or hardware demand,

- Xiaomi becomes primarily an interchangeable endpoint for third-party AI,
- service monetization remains structurally weak,
- EV losses become persistently destructive to group economics,
- geopolitical restrictions materially reduce international opportunity,
- the Human × Car × Home ecosystem fails to create meaningful user behavior change.

# 28. Ambiq Investment Case

Ambiq is the infrastructure expression of the same thesis.

The reasoning is:

Interconnected AI
→ more always-on nodes
→ more sensing and local inference
→ higher importance of battery life and thermal efficiency
→ greater demand for ultra-low-power edge compute.

Ambiq is particularly exposed to this chain because its company identity is already centered around ultra-low-power semiconductor solutions for edge AI and intelligent connected devices.

The opportunity is attractive because Ambiq does not need to predict whether Apple, Google, Meta, Xiaomi or another ecosystem wins.

If many ecosystems deploy more persistent wearables, sensors and edge intelligence, the addressable market can grow across all of them.

This is the classic infrastructure advantage:

> **sell into the transition rather than needing to predict the final consumer winner.**

# 29. Ambiq Risks and Kill Conditions

## Competitive Displacement

Larger semiconductor companies can close the efficiency gap or bundle sufficiently good low-power compute with broader platforms.

## Customer Concentration

A small company can appear diversified at the end-market level while still depending heavily on a limited number of major customers.

## Atomiq Commercial Risk

Future AI-oriented products can be technologically impressive without generating major design wins.

## Valuation Risk

A high-growth semiconductor company can be operationally successful and still be a bad investment if expectations become too extreme.

## Small-Cap Execution Risk

Scaling supply, engineering, sales and customer support is difficult.

## Evidence That Would Strengthen the Position

- large new design wins,
- increasing edge-AI mix,
- continued revenue acceleration,
- durable gross-margin expansion,
- Atomiq production adoption,
- independent evidence of a persistent compute-per-joule advantage.

## Evidence That Would Weaken or Break the Position

- major customers move to competitors,
- power-efficiency differentiation disappears,
- revenue growth decouples from the broader edge-AI market,
- Atomiq fails to obtain material commercial adoption,
- larger vendors commoditize Ambiq's key advantage.

# 30. Why Xiaomi Is USD 15,000 and Ambiq Is USD 5,000

The position sizes are intentionally asymmetric.

Xiaomi receives the larger allocation because the company can benefit from the thesis through several independent paths:

- phones,
- wearables,
- home devices,
- HyperOS,
- AI,
- services,
- cars,
- chips,
- ecosystem lock-in.

The Interconnected AI thesis does not need every one of these to succeed.

Ambiq has a narrower but potentially highly convex exposure.

Its upside is substantial if low-power edge compute becomes a major bottleneck and Ambiq wins material share.

But the firm-specific probability distribution is wider.

The world thesis can be right while Ambiq loses.

That is why the initial nominal decision is:

> **Xiaomi: USD 15,000**

> **Ambiq: USD 5,000**

This also keeps the pilot-period sizing more consistent with the scale of earlier virtual investments instead of repeating the mistake of mechanically translating every conviction level into the later USD 1,000,000 formal framework before formal inception.

These allocations can be increased later only through a new dated decision if evidence improves.

# 31. Time Horizon

This transition does not need a decade before becoming visible.

A plausible progression is:

## 2026–2027

- cross-app agents,
- more persistent AI,
- early cross-device state,
- increasingly useful glasses,
- AI watches,
- home and car orchestration,
- first clearly recognizable distributed AI workflows.

## 2027–2029

The user increasingly experiences AI as persistent infrastructure rather than an application on one device.

Devices begin behaving like specialized parts of a common system.

## Later

Interconnection becomes normal infrastructure.

Robotics expands the system into general physical actuation.

For investors, complete adoption is unnecessary.

Markets can reprice companies as soon as evidence appears that their existing assets will capture meaningful value from the transition.

# 32. Central Investment Thesis

The thesis can ultimately be condensed into the following:

> **The next major AI transition may come less from another extreme increase in model intelligence and more from organizing already capable AI across applications, persistent context, wearables, vehicles, smart homes, sensors and eventually robots. Computing therefore shifts from isolated devices toward distributed, state-centric personal AI systems.**

A second implication follows:

> **As model intelligence becomes increasingly abundant, scarcity may migrate toward context, identity, permissions, distribution, sensor access, physical device graphs, human interfaces, actuators, orchestration infrastructure and energy-efficient edge compute.**

And critically:

> **The thesis does not predict that any particular company will win this transition. It predicts that companies already controlling strategically useful layers of this emerging architecture can benefit substantially as those layers become more valuable. Multiple competing companies can therefore profit from the same technological shift at the same time.**

The final investment question is therefore not simply:

> Who wins AI?

It is:

> **Which existing assets become dramatically more valuable when intelligence stops living inside individual applications and begins coordinating the digital and physical world as one persistent system — and which of those assets are not yet fully reflected in the security's price?**

---

# 33. Decision Summary

**Xiaomi — BUY, USD 15,000 virtual allocation.**

The investment expresses a high-conviction view that a broad physical device graph can become much more economically valuable when AI turns devices from isolated products into coordinated nodes. Xiaomi does not need to win the model war. It needs to capture enough incremental hardware, service, retention, ecosystem and platform value from the transition.

**Ambiq — BUY, USD 5,000 virtual allocation.**

The investment expresses a higher-risk infrastructure view that always-on AI will make power efficiency a more important semiconductor bottleneck. Ambiq does not need to control the consumer platform, but it must maintain meaningful technical differentiation and win commercial design share.

**No Alphabet sizing change is recorded in this document.**

Any future reduction, increase or re-underwriting of Alphabet will be documented separately as its own dated decision.
