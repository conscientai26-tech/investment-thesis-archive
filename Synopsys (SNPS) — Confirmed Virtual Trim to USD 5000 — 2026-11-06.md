## Why the decision is reasonable

This decision extends, rather than rewrites, the [14 October 2026 SNPS/CDNS reduction memo](./Cadence%20(CDNS)%20Exit%20and%20Synopsys%20(SNPS)%20Trim%20%E2%80%94%20Investment%20Decision%20%E2%80%94%202026-10-14.md). That contemporaneous memo estimated five-year annualized SNPS return of 9.2% versus a 7.5% assumed benchmark, or only **+1.7 annual percentage points** of scenario-weighted expected alpha. Those figures were subjective scenarios, not empirically calibrated or audited returns.

The thesis remains credible: high switching costs and validation barriers in EDA, expanding demand for advanced chip design, and a broadening engineering market after the Ansys acquisition. The bear case remains primarily **Ansys integration / accounting and cash-conversion quality / debt / pricing and growth durability**. The broader AI-credit shock is only indirectly relevant to EDA demand. At a lower share price, unchanged earnings/terminal assumptions can actually increase expected return. A further trim is therefore a portfolio risk-budget decision, **not** a claim the business is failing or the stock must fall.

User indicated the USD 7,000 position was approximately **11% in profit** as of the discussion. Treat this as the user's reported performance figure; it does not establish audited holdings, entry basis after the November 5 full virtual re-entry, or actual realizable proceeds. Do not calculate or log realized gain from unverified fills. The decision does not require the position to be above its entry price to be logically valid; profit since purchase is not a valuation measure.
